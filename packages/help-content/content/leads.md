# Your prospects and how they're scored

The Prospects page shows every prospect your Prospect Agent sourced. Each prospect goes through two checks: a rules gate that confirms basic fit with your ideal customer profile, and an AI ranking that scores nuanced fit from 0–100 with a written rationale.

The rules gate reads job titles the way a person would: "Chief Executive Officer" counts as CEO, "Vice President of Sales" as VP of Sales, and "Director of Sales & Business Development" as a Sales Director, whatever the word order. Leadership levels of the same function are treated alike, so a Head of, VP, or Director of Sales all fit a "Sales Director" target. A target that is only a level, like "VP", still requires that level. The AI ranking scores a prospect 70+ when their role clearly fits your ideal customer profile and nothing contradicts it. It doesn't mark someone down for missing company details or for not having a recent buying signal, and it only weighs criteria you actually set, such as location only when you've named a location.

Each prospect leads with the reason they're here. The row shows three **fit bars** and then the score: three bars is a hot prospect (85+), two is a strong fit (70+), and one is worth a look. A LinkedIn mark sits on each prospect.

The **Why now** column shows the newest buying signal with when it happened and, when there is one, a link to the post behind it. If there are more, you'll see **+N more**. Signals are real ones captured during enrichment (a funding round, an executive hire, a new office, a product launch, active buying intent); when there isn't one, you see the AI's read on the prospect's top trigger or pain. When a prospect has replied but the reply has been sitting for a few days, it's marked **cooling** so a warm conversation never quietly goes cold. On wider screens you'll also see **Worth** (what closing that prospect means toward your revenue goal) and **Last activity**.

**Hot right now** sits at the top of the page: your strongest-fit prospects, pulled out of the list so you can work the best opportunities first.

The tabs show counts. **Scheduled** lists prospects who are in an active sequence, so you can see who is already being worked.

You can **search** the list by name, company, or title, and narrow it with **Filters**: industry, minimum score, when the prospect was sourced, in-market only, or a **Signal** group (each signal type with a count). Sort by **Score**, **Company**, or **Last activity** (click a column header; click again to go back to newest first), choose how many prospects show per page (10, 25, or 50), and jump to any page. The table remembers your comfortable or compact row-density choice.

To work many prospects at once, tick the **checkboxes** and use the bulk bar: **Archive** stops outreach to the selected prospects and cancels their queued drafts (history kept), and **Suppress** permanently adds them to your never-contact list. Both ask you to confirm first.

## The side peek

Click any prospect to open a **side peek** without losing your place in the list. Use **←** and **→** to move to the previous or next prospect. The peek has collapsible sections:

- **Signals** — what happened and when.
- **Why this rank** — what lifted or lowered the score.
- **Why this prospect** — the AI's rationale, pain points, and buying triggers.
- **Company** — firmographics and their LinkedIn profile.
- **Message** — the queued draft. Read it, edit it, or regenerate it with a note to steer the rewrite, right from the peek.
- **Fit** — **Good fit** or **Not a fit**, with an optional reason (see below).
- **CRM** — the connection status and any record created.
- **Notes** — your team's notes.

The footer has **Not a fit** and **Open full brief**, the complete page with "Your opening" (the angle most likely to land), the full activity timeline, and deal controls. Prospects that didn't fit are under "Filtered out", each with the reasons.

When a newly qualified prospect carries a fresh, high-value signal (a funding round, an executive hire, an acquisition, or active buying intent) you'll get a **hot-signal notification** in the bell, so you can pounce while the timing is right.

## What's working, and why a prospect ranks where it does

Once your agent has enough invitations with a fair chance at a reply, the top of the Prospects page shows **What's working**: the kinds of prospects replying more and less than your average — for example "Funding signal 2.1×" (prospects with a recent funding round reply at about twice your usual rate) or "Managers 0.5×". These come only from your own results, never anyone else's.

Open any prospect and, under its score, you'll see **Ranked higher** / **Ranked lower** with the reasons that apply to that person. Your agent uses the same evidence to choose who gets each day's limited invitations. It never changes who qualifies — the 70+ score bar stays exactly where it is.

Nothing shows here for a new account; it appears once there's real evidence (roughly 30 invitations at least two weeks old).

## Good fit / Not a fit

Open any prospect and tell your agent what you think with **Good fit** or **Not a fit**. Any workspace member can do this.

- **Not a fit** (you can add a reason, which is optional) keeps that person out of your outreach — your agent won't draft a message for them — and teaches the scoring to rank similar people lower.
- **Good fit** teaches it to look for more people like this one.
- Changed your mind? **Undo** clears it, and a "Not a fit" prospect becomes eligible for outreach again.

These verdicts work alongside the **Wrong person** reason you can pick when rejecting a draft in Approvals.

## Adding a prospect yourself

**Add prospect** (top right) lets you bring your own prospect: first name and their LinkedIn profile URL are required; title and company are optional but help the scoring judge fit. They enter the exact same funnel as sourced prospects — Vera researches and scores them against your ICP, and only a qualifying score moves them into outreach drafting. Adding someone by hand is never a shortcut past the quality bar, so a hand-added prospect can still land in "Filtered out" with the reasons. Profiles on your suppression list can't be added, and a profile that's already a prospect won't be duplicated. Adding prospects is an admin action.

## Correcting a prospect

On a prospect's full profile, the pencil next to their name lets you **edit** their first/last name, title, and company — useful when research got something wrong or a prospect changed roles. Corrected details feed the next drafted message directly. Editing is admin-only.

## Notes

The **Notes** panel on the full profile keeps your own knowledge with the prospect — context from a call, a mutual connection, a reason to wait. Any teammate can read and add notes; each note shows its author and date, and you can remove your own. Notes are for your team — they're never sent to the prospect.

Only high-quality prospects (score 70+ by default) move on to outreach drafting. Prospects that never qualify are automatically removed after 90 days.
