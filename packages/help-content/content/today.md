# Today — your five-second answer

**Today** is your home — it's where you land every time you sign in. It is not an analytics page: it exists to answer five questions in order, top to bottom, and then hand you off to the work.

1. **Is anything broken?** A banner appears only when something has actually stopped the engine — a LinkedIn sender that dropped or was restricted, or a payment that failed. No banner means nothing is stopping it.
2. **What needs me?** The greeting's one sentence, the dark button beside it, and the **Needs you** row.
3. **Is it working?** Four numbers for the last seven days.
4. **What exactly is waiting?** The work card, with your drafts, replies, and activity.
5. **What did it do while I was gone?** The Activity tab.

## The status sentence

Under your greeting is a single sentence of fact — what the engine did since your last visit, and whether your senders are inside their limits. It changes with the state of your account: overnight runs read "Overnight the engine drafted 14 messages and 3 people replied"; a mid-day visit reads "Since 6:30am…". When there is nothing waiting, it tells you when the next drafts are expected instead. On a brand-new account, while the first pass is still running, it reads "Finding prospects · first batch in about N min".

## Needs you

Up to four items, in priority order, each one click from done:

- **Repairs first** — a disconnected or restricted sender, or a failed payment. These stop sending, so they outrank everything.
- **No LinkedIn connected yet** — shown when no LinkedIn account is connected. Your agent is already finding prospects, but nothing can send until you connect one, and the tile links to **Senders**.
- **Your queue and your replies** — how many drafts are waiting, and how many people are waiting on an answer.
- **Specific threads** — a conversation the agent handed back to you because it reached its limit, or a meeting happening today.
- **Occasional suggestions** — a pattern in what you have been rejecting, an edit habit worth saving as a rule, or a one-time setup ask. These can be dismissed and only return on a new trigger.

When nothing needs you, the row says so in one line and the dark button disappears. That is the intended end state, not an empty page.

## Get started

New accounts see a **Get started** checklist on Today. Four steps count toward your progress, in this order:

1. **Confirm your buyers.** Done when you save your targeting in **Playbook → Targeting**.
2. **Review first prospects.** Done when you mark any prospect **Good fit** or **Not a fit**, or reject a draft with a reason.
3. **Connect LinkedIn.** Done when you have an active LinkedIn sender.
4. **Approve your first message.** Done when you approve your first draft.

There is also an optional fifth step, **Connect calendar or CRM**. It is done when you have an active scheduling or CRM connection, and it never counts toward the four.

Once two steps are done, the checklist collapses to a small progress pill. You can dismiss it only after all four steps are done, and once dismissed it does not come back.

## The four numbers

**Sent**, **Replies**, **Interested**, and **Meetings** — each over the last seven days, each with one line of context underneath. Reply rate is only shown once you have at least twenty sends, because below that it is noise rather than a rate. There are no deltas, arrows, or charts here on purpose; the weekly email covers trends. Each number opens where it lives: **Sent** opens what's sending in Approvals, **Replies** and **Interested** open the Inbox, and **Meetings** opens your meetings list.

## The work card

One card, three tabs:

- **Queue** — the drafts waiting for you, strongest first (replies to a live conversation come first of all). Each row shows who, the signal that makes them worth reaching now, their score, which step of the sequence this is, and **when it would send if you approved it now**. Click a row to open the draft.
- **Replies** — people waiting on an answer, oldest first, because the oldest one is the one going cold. A reply the agent has already drafted an answer for is marked "Draft reply ready".
- **Activity** — what the engine did in the last 24 hours: what it scored, what it drafted, what it sent, and what you approved.

Hovering a queue row gives you three actions: **Later** (push it down this session), **Reject** (with a reason), and **Review** (open the draft). There is no Approve on the row itself — approving means you read it, and the row only shows the opening line. Rejecting asks for one reason: wrong person, bad timing, weak message, or other. Those reasons are what teach the engine which prospects to stop bringing you.

Keyboard: **↑ ↓** move between rows, **Enter** opens the draft, **L** is Later, **R** is Reject. Inside the draft: **A** approves, **E** edits, **G** cleans up a flagged draft, **R** rejects, and **← →** move through the queue without closing.

## The engine line

Along the bottom of the card, one line always tells you what the sending layer is doing: whether it is running, when the next message goes out and from which sender, how much of today's allowance is used, and the sending window. If something is wrong, this line says what and links to the fix.

The limits shown here are the real ones the system enforces — they are not settings. New or quiet accounts ramp up gradually over their first four weeks, and there is a hard weekly ceiling on connection invites. These protect your own LinkedIn account, so they cannot be raised.

## The status chip

Every page has a status chip in the top bar that says what your engine is doing right now: for example "Finding prospects · next run in ~25 min", "Sending · 4 of 20 invites today", "Paused by you", or "Ready" when there is nothing to report. When something needs you, it says so and gives you the fix: "LinkedIn not connected · Connect", "Payment failed · Fix". Click it to open a small panel with **Sourcing** (Prospect and Intent agents) and **Outreach** (Outreach agent) switches, and **Pause everything** / **Resume everything**. Pausing everything pauses every agent and sending; resuming turns them all back on. The switches are locked while paused.

## Common questions

- **Why is a draft "held"?** Its sender is disconnected or restricted. The draft is kept, not lost; it sends once that sender is back.
- **Why did my queue not fill up?** If the engine ran several passes and found nobody above your quality bar, Today tells you so and points at your profile — it is usually too narrow.
- **Can I pause everything?** Yes — the workspace menu at the top left has **Pause engine**. Sourcing and sending stop; your approvals stay open, and Today shows that it is paused until you resume it.
