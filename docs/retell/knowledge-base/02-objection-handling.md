# Objection Handling

Objections on a cold call are reflexes, not verdicts. Never argue, never speed up, never sound hurt.

## The universal method (LAER + soft ask)
Run this on every objection — never jump straight to a rebuttal.
1. Listen — let them finish completely. Don't talk over the objection.
2. Acknowledge — validate it sincerely: "Totally fair." / "I'd probably say the same."
3. Explore — one calibrated question to find what's really behind it. The stated objection often isn't the real one.
4. Respond — one short reframe. No paragraphs, no "but...".
5. Soft ask — the smallest next step (callback, one-line email, 10-minute slot).
If declined clearly twice, exit warm and stop.

## Tone rules
- Match calm to their heat. If they get sharp, get softer and slower.
- A "no" is information, not rejection. Stay relaxed and human.

## Classic patterns
- Feel / Felt / Found: "I understand how you feel — a lot of {role}s felt that way too. What they found was {outcome}." Use sparingly.
- Label the resistance: "It sounds like you've been burned by a tool like this before." Naming an emotion drains it.
- Accusation audit: "You're probably thinking this is just another cold call — and you might be right. One line to prove otherwise?"
- Mirror: repeat their last few words as a question: "No bandwidth right now?"

## Objection library (acknowledge → reframe → soft ask)

### "I'm not interested."
"Totally fair — you can't be interested in something you've barely heard. One sentence, and if it lands flat I'll let you go?" → sharpest pain line → "Worth a quick look, or genuinely not for you?"

### "Just send me an email."
"Happy to — so I don't send a wall of text you'll never read, what's the one thing worth including?" → "I'll keep it to three lines. If it's useful, does 15 minutes next week make sense?"
If they insist: "I'll send it today and call Thursday at 10 to see if it's worth pursuing — fair?"

### "We already have a solution / we use {competitor}."
"Good — most people I call do, and I'm not here to rip out what works. The {role}s who took the meeting anyway were curious about {gap}. Worth 15 minutes just to confirm you're ahead?"

### "We don't have budget."
"Makes sense, and I'm not asking you to spend anything — the meeting's free, no pitch to buy. If {pain} is costing you {implication}, isn't 15 minutes worth it before next year's budget?"

### "Now's not a good time / I'm busy."
"I figured — I called out of the blue. When's actually better, tomorrow morning or afternoon?" → set a concrete time (callback).

### "How did you get my number? / Is this a sales call?"
"Fair question — it's a business call, yeah. I found you because you're {role} at {company}, which is exactly who I tend to be useful to. Thirty seconds to tell you why, then you decide?"

### "Send me your pricing."
"I don't want to throw a number that's wrong for your setup — that's what the 15 minutes is for. Rough size of the team this would touch?" → use to justify the meeting.

### "I need to talk to my team / I'm not the right person."
"Totally — who else would care about {area}? Worth me grabbing 15 minutes with both of you so you don't relay it secondhand? When are you usually around?"

### "We tried something like this and it didn't work."
"That's exactly why I'd want to understand what broke first, so I don't waste your time repeating it. What went wrong last time?" → listen → "Sounds like {root cause}. Worth 15 minutes to see if that's avoidable here?"

### "Call me back in 3 / 6 months."
"Happy to. So it's a fit but not now — what changes in {timeframe} that makes it the right moment?" → if real, set the date (callback). If vague, one gentle test, then respect it.

### "Take me off your list / don't call me again."
Stop selling immediately. No reframe, no last pitch.
"Of course — I'll remove you right now and you won't hear from me again. Sorry to catch you at a bad time. Take care."

---

## When to stop
- Two clear declines → exit warm.
- Any do-not-call / remove-me request → exit immediately, confirm removal.
- Pushing past a genuine no costs more than the meeting is worth. A graceful exit sometimes earns the inbound later.
